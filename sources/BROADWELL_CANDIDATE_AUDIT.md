# Broadwell dictionary check of three exploratory Timucua–Muskogean comparisons

Source checked: George Aaron Broadwell, *A Timucua–English–Spanish Dictionary*, draft September 2024. Page numbers below refer to the PDF stored at the repository root.

This is a source audit of the Timucua side of three previously proposed comparisons. It is not a cognacy or borrowing judgment.

| Proposed comparison | What the dictionary actually records | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Timucua *efa* “dog” ~ Muskogean *ifa* | *efa* [efa] “dog,” labelled **Confident** (p. 86). | The Timucua form and meaning are well supported. This does not by itself prove borrowing or its direction. The proposed Muskogean donor form and the historical claim about transfer still need independent source verification. |
| Timucua *neha* “grease” ~ Muskogean *niha* “grease” | *neha* [neha] “sauce/grease; medicinal liquid” (p. 198; no status label printed). *niha* [niha] means “older sibling” and is labelled **Probable** (p. 201). | The proposed semantic match is not supported by this dictionary: the Timucua entry has the food/liquid meaning, while the Timucua *niha* entry means “older sibling.” Do not count this as evidence for a loanword without a different, independently sourced Muskogean form. |
| Timucua *ilake* “night” ~ Muskogean *niɬaki* “night” | The headword is *ilaqe* [ilake], “become night, afternoon”; the same entry also lists noun “night,” labelled **Confident** (p. 138). *ylaqi* [ilaki] is listed as a spelling variant (p. 297). | The Timucua form and “night” meaning are supported. The comparison remains a hypothesis: the Muskogean form, sound correspondences, direction, and chronology need separate verification. |

## Research implications

1. Retain *efa* and *ilaqe* as source-supported Timucua forms for further comparison, not as established loans.
2. Remove *neha–niha* from the current candidate evidence set unless an independent Muskogean source supplies the proposed “grease” form.
3. For every surviving candidate, record the exact source form, meaning, citation, confidence, proposed donor language, and sound changes. Require regular correspondences and chronological plausibility before assigning a borrowing label.

## Source
